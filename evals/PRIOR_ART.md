# Prior art and claims to avoid: context for the Validity, Creativity and sceptic judges

From research/prior_art_space_medicine.md (2 Nov 2026; secondary sources, so verify quotes before they go on slides).
At global judging, a NASA expert who knows these systems leads scoring. Penalise any claim on the avoid list.

## 5. Positioning lines (five things we can say truthfully)

1. "NASA's Exploration Medical Capability team has already defined the need for an onboard clinical decision support system for Earth-independent medical operations. Our prototype is a hackathon-scale mock-up of one crew-facing slice of that concept: routine self-monitoring and early escalation."
2. "Wearables like CSA's Bio-Monitor already capture continuous vital signs on the ISS. We don't add sensors. We add a transparent layer that compares readings with each astronaut's own baseline and says, with a cited source, what to do next."
3. "NASA's current AI prototypes, like the Crew Medical Officer Digital Assistant, are LLM-centred. We deliberately keep the escalation decision in fixed, reviewable rules, and use AI only to explain. That way every alert can be traced to its source by a flight surgeon."
4. "Our delay-aware escalation follows the Earth-independent medical operations framing NASA and CSA already use. The new part is showing the crew how long a reply from Earth will take and what to do in the meantime."
5. "Everything you see runs on open and clearly labelled synthetic data. It shows the workflow works, not that it's clinically accurate. Validation would follow NASA's own path: scenario-based testing with physician evaluators, as the CMO-DA team did."

## 6. Claims to avoid

- "First" or "novel" for any of these: AI medical assistant for astronauts (CMO-DA, Doc-in-a-Box), onboard CDSS (ExMC CDS), autonomous guided ultrasound (AMOS, Butterfly, ADUM), astronaut wearables (Astroskin, SmartTex), a deep-space health challenge (CSA DSHC), or the remote-community spin-off (CSA Health Beyond).
- That NASA "has no" decision support or "isn't working on" autonomy. Gaps Med07, Med10 and Medical-701 have funded projects against them.
- Any clinical accuracy, sensitivity or specificity figure, or "validated" or "clinically tested". Planted synthetic anomalies only prove the plumbing works.
- "Diagnoses" or "replaces the flight surgeon or CMO". NASA frames CDSS as augmenting the CMO.
- Integration or compatibility with NASA systems (MDA, CHP-IDA, Chiron, ISS Medical Checklist, CHeCS) or with Bio-Monitor data, unless we actually show it.
- Using real astronaut, Artemis II, Polaris Dawn, Fram2 or EXPAND data. None of it is open. Only OSD-569 (Inspiration4) is.
- Mission risk numbers presented as coming from IMM or IMPACT. IMPACT results are explicitly preliminary.
- Exact ISS or Artemis protocols or kit contents, beyond what's public and cited.
- "NASA standards say X" without the clause in hand. That includes the vital-sign trending line until it's verified.
- CMO-DA scores presented as a benchmark we beat. Their scores come from 3 simulated cases; we have none.

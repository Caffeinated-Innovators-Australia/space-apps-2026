# Winning traits: what Space Apps judges have rewarded

Distilled from research/past_winners_insights.md (2016–2025 winners) and audited on 14 Nov 2026 against the live official
2026 Judging & Awards and Project Submission guides. This checklist is our own diagnostic. It is not an official criterion and no official judge uses it. Judges in the judge-evals panel read this before scoring. The `winning_traits`
checklist judge scores each item pass/fail.

Key facts for judges:
- Five criteria, each scored 1 to 5 at every level of judging (2026 guide). The guide warns that teams neglect **Presentation**, which is judged on the demo and the full project page.
- At global judging, expert judges review Global Nominees, then an executive committee from NASA and partner agencies picks winners (2026 guide). Expect a domain-expert reader.
- At least one NASA data source or resource is **required for Global Award eligibility**, and Relevance rewards NASA data used as a core component (2026 guides).
- The demo is a video of 30 s or less, or 7 or fewer slides including title and intro. **A demo over the limit is not eligible for Global Judging** (2026 guide). Most of the evaluation rests on the project page.
- Almost every 2023–25 global winner showed a working artefact (an app, dashboard, model or game), not just a concept.
- Closest analogue: SpaceGenes+ (2025, Best Use of Science). It was a dashboard over a named NASA GeneLab dataset (GLDS-288) on astronaut health.
  Astronaut vitals monitors from 2018–2022 reached Global Nominee but didn't win, so the "act" layer is what tends to be missing.
- Most losses come from admin and rules, not ideas: the "Create Your Own Challenge" track, private links, missing English subtitles,
  unregistered or waitlisted members, uncited assets, undisclosed or unmarked AI use.

Official suggested video outline (2026 Project Submission Guide; a suggestion, not a scored requirement, and the guide does
not call it "30 Seconds of Glory"): 0:00 introduce yourself, your name and your team's name · 0:02 which challenge and what
inspired the choice · 0:05 the project's title and tagline · 0:10 how it works, with images or a prototype, a user's
experience, and how NASA and partner data made it possible · 0:20 to 0:30 the future, what it could do for people and the world.
A slide demo should carry the team name, the members' names and the challenge selected.

What official judges are shown: the project submission page, the demo link and the final project link. The Submission Guide says
most of the evaluation rests on the project page. The Use of SI answer is collected for data purposes and is not shown to judges.

| W20 | The user interface meets basic accessibility: colour is never the only alert cue, readable font sizes, plain-language text. | Global Connection (Arcobaleno 2023) [Snippet]; slide readability tip [Primary] |
| W21 | Every required 2026 field is filled in: Project Name, High-Level Summary, demo link, final project link, Detailed Description (what, how, benefits, tools), Use of SI, NASA Data, and Partner Data and Other References. | 2026 Submission Guide [Primary] |
| W22 | All judge-critical content lives on the project page itself, not only in a linked repository or document. | 2026 Submission Guide ("This is the page that the judges will review") [Primary] |
| W23 | The demo uses strong visuals (diagrams, screenshots, the product itself) and keeps text per slide to roughly 30 words or fewer. | 2026 Submission Guide (strong visuals, readability, limit the words per slide) [Primary]; the word count is [Inference] |
| W24 | A clinician-credible reviewer (team or local judge) would find no unsafe advice in the demo path. | Melbourne 2024 panel included a medical academic [Snippet]; Validity [Primary]; [Inference] |

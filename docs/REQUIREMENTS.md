# Requirements: astronaut health decision support (working title)

Version: v1.1 · Status: building · Owner: Build lead · Last lint: 14 Nov 2026, see docs/DECISION_LOG.md

> Source of truth for WHAT we build. Every build task cites an ID from here.

## 1. Product in one line
- **Pitch:** A delay-aware health decision-support tool for a long mission. It asks for simple check-ins through the day, compares each with the astronaut's own baseline, spots slow trends that a single reading hides, explains every signal in plain words, and counts down to Earth's earliest reply when a case must go.
- **Primary user:** One astronaut on a Mars-class mission, not a medical expert, doing a daily check-in alone.
- **Secondary users:** The crew medic; the flight surgeon on Earth who receives the case later.
- **Fifteen indicators in seven groups:** Mind and sleep (sleep, mood, reaction time); Heart (resting heart rate, blood pressure upper and lower); Eyes (near vision); Muscle and bone (exercise plan done, grip strength, body mass); Food and fluid (fluid intake, food energy); Blood (haemoglobin); Cabin and radiation (cabin carbon dioxide, radiation dose). Six are daily (sleep, resting heart rate, mood, exercise plan done, fluid intake, food energy). Blood pressure, near vision, grip strength, body mass and haemoglobin are weekly, each due on its own day of the mission week. Reaction time (a tap test inside the app) is asked for when the rules call for it. Cabin carbon dioxide and radiation dose come from sensors, SYNTHETIC in the demo.
- **Mission contexts covered:** Five environment profiles: Mars base, In transit, Moon base, Space station and Remote Australia. Each changes the reply delay, who answers, the dominant risks shown and the guidance line. Each place also carries its gravity, cabin atmosphere and how far help is (FR-48). The rules themselves do not yet change by environment; that is a clinician decision (CR-17).

## 2. Demo story: "Day 214 on a Mars base" (judges see this end to end in under a minute)
1. The day starts with its prompts. The scheduler holds the check-ins due during the EVA (extravehicular activity, a spacewalk or surface traverse, 08:00 to 12:00) until five minutes after it ends, and re-plans when the EVA runs 45 minutes late: needs FR-19
2. Jo logs sleep. Every night of the past three weeks sat inside their own range, but the 7-reading average has slid. The tool flags the trend and prompts a reaction-time check: needs FR-01, FR-02, FR-18
3. Cabin carbon dioxide reads 3.4 mmHg, above NASA's cabin limit of 3 mmHg. The tool rates it Act now and cites the NASA limit: needs FR-18, DR-02
4. The astronaut does the reaction tap test (five rounds, about thirty seconds). The result is compared with their own baseline: needs FR-01
5. Each signal has an explanation card (how to measure, why it matters, what to do, NASA hazards, sources), and the three flagged signals are shown together on one card, each with a tier as colour, shape and word: needs FR-03, FR-04, FR-18, FR-21
6. Numbered steps say what to do now. One tap sends the case pack, and a countdown shows the earliest Earth reply: needs FR-05, FR-06, FR-09
7. Three days of low fluid raise a Watch early warning. Pain starts, and the matched pattern is Act now, with a urine test strip and guided ultrasound: needs FR-28, FR-27, FR-40, FR-41
8. That night a fever with low blood pressure and fast breathing raises a possible-sepsis alert, which opens emergency mode on the module's sepsis pathway with Jo's readings: needs FR-38, FR-39, FR-44
9. The same rules are shown on real crew data, labelled "Data: NASA OSDR, OSD-569", and the place switcher shows the same case at a Simpson Desert station 640 km from the nearest hospital: needs DR-01, FR-22

## 4. Out of scope (explicit)
- A language model at run time: explanations are written text filled from each rule's own values, so the demo is offline and rules decide.
- Voice input on our own screens, connections to real sensors or devices (the cabin carbon dioxide and radiation dose readings, and the vital signs, are SYNTHETIC in the demo) and a rover profile. None of these is built. The emergency module has its own optional voice input, off until tapped (FR-44).
- Capping drift to Watch while adapting to new gravity, and re-baselining after it (a clinician decision).
- Capturing or keeping any ultrasound image, or saying what an image shows.
- Any calibrated or clinical eye test (for example retinal imaging). The near-vision self-check only compares the person with their own earlier results.
- Any direct bone measurement. Exercise and grip strength stand in for bone.
- Rules that change by environment (a clinician decision, CR-17).
- Accounts; a server or shared record across devices; rankings or scores between crew.
- Any diagnosis, and any clinical accuracy claim.

## 5. Assumptions and open questions
- A1: The fifteen indicators cover the main spaceflight health areas, at the team lead's request (D-015), and are confirmed by the clinicians (CLINICIAN_REVIEW.md, CR-01). Until then they are marked placeholders. The team's challenge brief advises five to seven indicators.
- A2: OSD-569 holds pre-flight and post-flight draws only (three before launch, four after return, four crew, a three-day mission). We show it as real personal baselines and recovery, never as in-flight data.
- A3: The Moon and Mars delays shown are light-time arithmetic, labelled as such. The remote Australia retrieval time is a SYNTHETIC scenario.
- A4: Every rule number (band factor 2, trend window 7, trend shift 1 standard deviation, sustained count 3, combined minimum of 2 flagged signals) is a statistical convention and a placeholder until a clinician signs it (CR-02, CR-16).
- A5: The cabin carbon dioxide limit of 3 mmHg (one-hour average, NASA-STD-3001 Volume 2) is a habitat design requirement, not a clinical alert threshold. Using it as a prompt is the team's choice.
- A6: The grip strength floor (in-mission skeletal muscle strength at or above 80% of baseline) and the career radiation limit (600 mSv) come from NASA-STD-3001 Volume 1. They are programme requirements, not clinical alert thresholds. Using them as prompts is the team's choice. Dose from earlier flights is not counted.
- A7: The monitor alert thresholds (SEP-01, SHK-01), the oxygen saturation and cabin pressure display bands, the lapse time, the adapting period, the urine strip note and the ultrasound and pathway step wording come from the clinical lead's build pack v3.3 (14 Nov 2026). Each is a placeholder until signed off (CLINICIAN_REVIEW.md, CR-36 onward).

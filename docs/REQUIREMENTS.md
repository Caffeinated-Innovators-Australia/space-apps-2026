# Requirements: astronaut health decision support (working title)

Version: v0.4 · Status: building · Owner: Build lead · Last lint: 14 Nov 2026, see docs/DECISION_LOG.md

> Source of truth for WHAT we build. Every build task cites an ID from here.

## 1. Product in one line
- **Pitch:** A delay-aware health decision-support tool for a long mission. Each day it asks for six simple check-ins, compares each with the astronaut's own baseline, spots slow trends that a single reading hides, explains every signal in plain words, and counts down to Earth's earliest reply when a case must go.
- **Primary user:** One astronaut on a Mars-class mission, not a medical expert, doing a daily check-in alone.
- **Secondary users:** The crew medic; the flight surgeon on Earth who receives the case later.
- **Six indicators:** Sleep, resting heart rate, mood, reaction time (a real 30-second tap test inside the app), cabin carbon dioxide (from a cabin sensor; SYNTHETIC in the demo) and haemoglobin (weekly).
- **Mission contexts covered:** Five environment profiles: Mars base (fully built), In transit, Moon base, Space station and Remote Australia. Each changes the reply delay, who answers, the dominant risks shown and the guidance line. The rules themselves do not yet change by environment; that is a clinician decision (CR-17).

## 2. Demo story: "Day 87 on a Mars base" (judges see this end to end in under a minute)
1. The day starts with its prompts. The scheduler holds the check-ins due during the EVA (extravehicular activity, a spacewalk or surface traverse, 08:00 to 12:00) until it ends, and re-plans when the EVA runs 45 minutes late: needs FR-19
2. Crew member 1 logs sleep. Every night of the past three weeks sat inside their own range, but the 7-reading average has slid. The tool flags the trend and prompts a reaction-time check: needs FR-01, FR-02, FR-18
3. Cabin carbon dioxide reads 3.4 mmHg, above NASA's cabin limit of 3 mmHg. The tool prompts the same reaction-time check: needs FR-18, DR-02
4. The astronaut does the 30-second tap test. The result is compared with their own baseline: needs FR-01
5. Each signal has an explanation card (how to measure, why it matters, what to do, NASA hazards, sources), and the flagged signals are shown together as one picture with a tier as colour, shape and word: needs FR-03, FR-04, FR-18, FR-21
6. Numbered steps say what to do now. One tap sends the case pack, and a countdown shows the earliest Earth reply: needs FR-05, FR-06, FR-09
7. The same rules are shown on real crew data, labelled "Data: NASA OSDR, OSD-569", and the environment switcher shows the same case in remote Australia: needs DR-01, FR-22

## 4. Out of scope (explicit)
- A language model at run time: explanations are written text filled from each rule's own values, so the demo is offline and rules decide.
- Voice input, connections to real sensors or devices (the cabin carbon dioxide reading is SYNTHETIC in the demo), a rover profile and the vision self-check. None of these is built.
- Rules that change by environment (a clinician decision, CR-17).
- Accounts; a server or shared record across devices; rankings or scores between crew.
- Any diagnosis, and any clinical accuracy claim.

## 5. Assumptions and open questions
- A1: The six indicators come from the team's challenge brief list and are confirmed by the clinicians (CLINICIAN_REVIEW.md, CR-01). Until then they are marked placeholders.
- A2: OSD-569 holds pre-flight and post-flight draws only (three before launch, four after return, four crew, a three-day mission). We show it as real personal baselines and recovery, never as in-flight data.
- A3: The Moon and Mars delays shown are light-time arithmetic, labelled as such. The remote Australia retrieval time is a SYNTHETIC scenario.
- A4: Every rule number (band factor 2, trend window 7, trend shift 1 standard deviation, sustained count 3) is a statistical convention and a placeholder until a clinician signs it (CR-02, CR-16).
- A5: The cabin carbon dioxide limit of 3 mmHg (one-hour average, NASA-STD-3001 Volume 2) is a habitat design requirement, not a clinical alert threshold. Using it as a prompt is the team's choice.

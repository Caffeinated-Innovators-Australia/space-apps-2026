# Hazard log

| # | Hazard | Effect | Control | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H-001 | A placeholder threshold is read as clinical advice | A user trusts an unreviewed value | Placeholders are visibly marked, carry no review badge, and are listed in CLINICIAN_REVIEW.md | Open |
| H-002 | Wording reads as a diagnosis | False reassurance or alarm | Assessment wording only; clinicians review every line | Open |
| H-003 | Synthetic data read as real | Loss of trust | SYNTHETIC label on every synthetic card and chart | Open |
| H-004 | Real crew data read as in-flight measurements | Misleading claim | On-screen text states pre-flight and post-flight | Open |
| H-005 | A normal reading raises an alert (false alarm) | Alert fatigue; crew stop trusting the tool | Every raised tier includes "Repeat the reading to confirm" (the first step for amber, the second for red). The result and the case pack say whether a second reading has confirmed it. One reading outside the band is amber; red needs three in a row outside the band (SU-01), or one reading beyond a sourced limit such as the NASA cabin carbon dioxide limit (LM-01). Whether one reading should raise a tier is a clinician decision (CR-12) | Open |
| H-006 | A real change stays inside the band (missed change) | False reassurance | The trend rule (TR-01) flags a slow change even when every reading is inside the band, and the trend chart is always shown beside the tier; the band and trend numbers await clinician review (CR-02, CR-16) | Open |
| H-007 | Crew wait for Earth instead of acting | Delay in care | Gap screen states how long a reply takes and tells the crew to start the steps now | Open |
| H-008 | Earth-mode wording read as clinical guidance for remote patients | Misuse outside the demo | SYNTHETIC label and placeholder note on screen; README states prototype only | Open |
| H-009 | A prompt arrives during a critical operation (for example an EVA) | A check-in distracts the astronaut at a dangerous moment | The prompt scheduler never prompts during a critical operation, holds those check-ins until five minutes after it ends, and re-plans when the task runs late (the demo slips the EVA by 45 minutes) | Open |
| H-010 | Streak rewards hide problems | Crew log good numbers to keep a streak, so real changes go unreported | The streak counts check-ins, never results. "Nothing to report" is a valid one-tap log. The crew goal has no ranking | Open |
| H-011 | Mistrust about who can see the data reduces honest reporting | Crew under-report mood, sleep or symptoms | The "Who sees what" table on the "Privacy and limits" screen states who sees each kind of data. It is a draft policy for the team to confirm (CR-13) | Open |
| H-012 | The near-vision self-check is read as an eye test | A person or medic trusts it to rule an eye problem in or out | The reading screen and the explanation card say it is not a calibrated eye chart and not a test for any eye condition; it only compares the person with their own earlier results on this device | Open |
| H-013 | An uncalibrated screen, a different distance or poor lighting changes the near-vision result | A false drop or a missed drop in lines read | The on-screen wording asks for arm's length (about 40 cm), usual glasses and the same device, distance and screen brightness each time; the card asks for a repeat in good light before telling the crew medic | Open |
| H-014 | The kidney stone pathway is read as a diagnosis, or its silence as an all-clear | A person with another cause of pain is falsely reassured or alarmed | Every card and case says "a pattern to check, not a diagnosis"; the case names what was checked for, not what was found; the follow-up screen says pain here has many causes. Symptoms, tiers and steps are placeholders (CR-25, CR-26) | Open |
| H-015 | A person in severe pain is asked to work through screens | Delay in telling the crew medic | The first step is always to tell the crew medic; the pain score is one tap; nothing must be completed before the case can be sent once the pattern is matched | Open |
| H-016 | The Emergency screen is followed as if it were approved for spaceflight | Basic life support steps written for Earth are applied where they may not hold, such as at Mars gravity | Every step is marked placeholder wording; the source and its date are on screen; nothing is said about low gravity, restraint, medicines or defibrillator settings; listed for clinicians as CR-32 | Open |
| H-017 | The early warning is read as a finding | A person believes they have a kidney stone because their fluid was low | The card says "a risk to act on early, not a finding"; no case can be sent for it; rule KS-00 is a placeholder (CR-29) | Open |
| H-018 | The crew medic overview or Earth inbox shows more than the privacy table allows | Crew stop reporting honestly | The overview is built only from sent cases, confirmed Act now results and totals, and a test fails if a raw value, mood or symptom label appears in it | Open |
| H-019 | A remote doctor's reply is mistaken for the app's advice | Responsibility is blurred | Replies are shown under the doctor's decision and time, in the doctor's own words; the app pre-fills and suggests nothing | Open |
| H-020 | An alert from one set of vital signs is wrong either way: a missed sepsis, or a false alarm from a typing slip or one odd reading | Delay in care, or alarm and lost trust | Plausibility bounds refuse typing slips; the alert lists the readings that raised it; a newer set clears it; the thresholds are flagged as placeholders (SEP-01, SHK-01); no alert is ever raised from oxygen saturation alone | Open |
| H-021 | Our rules and the emergency module disagree | The crew does not know which to follow | Our rules file decides the tier shown in our app. The module is opened from our alert and fed our readings; its own alerts are recorded and are not used to set a tier yet | Open |
| H-022 | The emergency module shows medicine names and doses that are not signed off | A crew member gives a medicine on unreviewed content | Labelled "not for clinical use" on our screen and inside the module; never shown on our own screens or in the video; listed for clinician review | Open |
| H-023 | Guided ultrasound is read as a finding | A person or medic trusts the app to say what an image means | The app never says what an image means, says "look for" only, and keeps no image | Open |
| H-024 | Crew check-in answers identify people | Crew stop answering honestly, or a person is singled out | Answers go to the flight psychologist only; the app exposes only a count of who has completed it, never an answer or an average (in a crew of four an average identifies people) | Open |
| H-025 | Voice input in the emergency module sends audio off the device in some browsers | Private speech leaves the device without the person knowing | Voice is off until tapped and optional; the module works by touch offline; this is stated in the documents | Open |
| H-026 | A symptom on the wider list, such as chest pain or short of breath, is logged and no rule answers it | The person reads silence as reassurance | Each area screen says most of these raise no alert yet and points to the crew medic and the Emergency button; the symptoms go with any case sent; listed for clinician rules (CR-51) | Open |
| H-027 | A record-only result (ECG, eye pressure, blood gas, cortisol) is read as checked | An abnormal result sits unseen | Every such card is marked "Record only"; the screen says nothing here raises an alert; results are sent with any case for the doctor to read; no threshold is invented (CR-52) | Open |
| H-028 | The Amsler grid or the heart ultrasound guide is taken as a test result | False reassurance | The grid records only what the person reports seeing; the ultrasound guide says the app does not read the image and keeps none; both are placeholders (CR-52, CR-53) | Open |
| H-029 | A free-text note carries a name or other private detail into a case | Private detail leaves the device | Notes are optional, capped at 200 characters, and the field says not to write anyone's name | Open |
| H-030 | An emergency symptom is tapped by mistake, or a serious symptom is not on the emergency list | A false emergency, or a real one with no banner | The banner says which symptom raised it and that tapping it again clears it; the Emergency button stays on every screen whatever is logged; the list is a placeholder for clinicians to extend (CR-59) | Open |
| H-031 | Speech recognition mishears a number | A wrong vital sign is saved, raising a false alert or hiding a real one | Every value is read back aloud and shown; nothing is saved until the person says save; the same possible-range check as typing applies; the source "by voice" is kept with the set and sent with the case | Open |
| H-032 | Speech recognition on our own screens sends audio off the device in some browsers | Private speech leaves the device without the person knowing | Off until tapped; stated on the Talk card and the Devices and AI screen; answers can be typed; every screen works by touch offline | Open |
| H-033 | A device reading is wrong, or comes from the wrong person's device | A wrong vital sign is saved | The screen names the person; each field shows which device supplied it; values can be corrected before saving; the browser's own chooser picks the device; the readers are tested against the published formats with example data; the demo kit is labelled SYNTHETIC | Open |
| H-034 | People read "AI" as the app diagnosing | Over-trust | The Devices and AI screen and the Talk card state what the AI does and never does; rules in the rules file decide every tier | Open |
| H-035 | A language model fills a wrong value or symptom from what was said | A wrong value is saved | Replies are checked against the app's own lists and possible ranges, every value is read back, and nothing is saved until "save" (FR-60) | Open |
| H-036 | A language model misses or invents an emergency | A delayed or needless emergency checklist | The emergency module's keywords back up the model for critical checklists; a suggestion is a button, never an automatic switch; "Press Emergency now" symptoms still raise the banner by rule (FR-61) | Open |
| H-037 | The AI clinical assistant gives unsafe or wrong advice, or a wrong dose | Harm | Off by default; every reply is labelled an AI suggestion; it is told the rules set every alert and to say "press Emergency" first; medicines are suggestions for the crew medic to check against the formulary; the crew medic decides (FR-62) | Open |
| H-038 | An API key or health data leaks | Privacy and security breach | Keys stay in the browser, never in the record, a case or the code; the assistant is sent only today's symptoms and latest vital signs; demo crew are SYNTHETIC | Open |

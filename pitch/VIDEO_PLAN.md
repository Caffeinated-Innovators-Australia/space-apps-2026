# Demo video plan

`demo-draft.mp4` is a draft cut of this plan: 28.9 seconds, 1920 by 1080, no sound, with English captions burned in. The official limit is 30 seconds. It is a screen recording of the live app with text cards. Every frame carries "Draft cut · assembled with AI assistance". It uses no outside footage, images or music.

The story, set by the team lead on 14 November 2026: **the first 20 seconds are a kidney stone on Mars; the last 9 seconds are the turn to Earth**, where the same gap exists for people in the remote outback.

## What the final cut still needs from the team

- **A voice.** A team member reads the captions as a voice-over. A real voice is better than a generated one. If a generated voice is used, say so in the AI-use statement.
- **Member names** on the opening card, if the team agrees. The public repository holds roles only.
- **The project name** on the title card.
- **Clinician sign-off** of the wording on the early warning, the pathway and the Urgent result. Until then every rule on screen shows its placeholder label, and the video shows it too.
- **A public link.** Host the final video where it opens with no sign-in, and put that link on the project page.
- **A check of the length.** The official limit is 30 seconds. This cut is 28.9.

## Rules this cut keeps

- English captions for every beat. SYNTHETIC is visible on every frame that shows health data.
- No NASA, agency or other organisation's logo. Sources are credited in text on the end card.
- No photo or footage of any person, and no outside images. The remote station is a SYNTHETIC scenario with no named place.
- Assessment wording only: "a pattern to check", "crew medic", "Urgent". Nothing says the app treats or cures.
- The 640 km figure is part of the SYNTHETIC scenario. The 252 against 88 and 7 million figures are from the Australian Institute of Health and Welfare.

## How it was made

The app was driven by a script in a real browser on the live site and recorded in one take. The scenes were cut from that take, captioned and joined with ffmpeg. The reply from Earth appears only after the demo's "skip ahead" button moves the mission clock on by the signal delay, so the clock on screen reads 07:21 when the reply lands.
